The government recorded 50 dengue deaths and 15,831 hospital admissions in the first nine days of October, as infections continued to rise following a sharp increase in September, according to the Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS).
The country reported 95,451 dengue cases and 295 deaths from January through October 9. A total of 89,380 patients had been discharged from hospitals, while 5,776 remained under treatment, DGHS data showed.
September was the deadliest month so far this year, with 43,774 hospital admissions and 148 deaths. Dengue deaths totalled five from January to May, 13 in June, 36 in July and 43 in August.
Hospital admissions also climbed from 9,206 in July to 20,536 in August before more than doubling in September. Admissions stood at 1,081 in January, 409 in February, 353 in March, 640 in April, 714 in May and 2,907 in June.
Former DGHS disease control director Professor Dr. Be-Nazir Ahmed said dengue transmission remained a risk in October and called for coordinated action by health authorities, local administrations and city corporations.
He also urged people not to ignore fever and to seek medical care promptly.
Doctors warned that continued rainfall, inadequate mosquito-breeding control and the spread of infections across regions could worsen the situation, although the scale of the outbreak by the end of October remained uncertain.
Cases rise outside Dhaka
Dengue infections have increasingly spread beyond the capital. According to DGHS data for the latest 24-hour reporting period, about 1,300 patients were admitted to hospitals nationwide, and a child died in Khulna. More than 90% of the newly admitted patients were from outside Dhaka, the data showed.
Admissions included 141 patients in Barishal division, 318 in Chattogram, 343 in Dhaka division outside the city corporations, 35 in Dhaka North City Corporation, 36 in Dhaka South City Corporation, 274 in Khulna, 69 in Mymensingh, 80 in Rangpur and four in Sylhet.
Although most patients have recovered and been discharged, the continued influx of new cases has put pressure on hospitals. Health officials have urged the public to seek timely treatment and strengthen efforts to eliminate mosquito-breeding sites.
